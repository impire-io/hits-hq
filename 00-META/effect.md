# Effect

Imagine that tomorrow we stand at the end of the HITS project. We have done everything we set out to do. How is working with it different from before?

## Tracking happens by itself

Agents file issues mid-flow: a bug noticed during a review, a follow-up deferred so it isn't lost, a task spun out of a conversation. Nobody stops working to go fill in a form somewhere else. The tracker is reachable from inside the work, so the record gets made at the moment the thing is noticed — not reconstructed at the end of the week, or forgotten.

## One question, one answer

"What is open? What is blocked, and on what? Who is on it?" is a lookup, not archaeology across chat threads, boards, and memory. Current state lives in a key-value projection that answers instantly, and search — by text, by meaning, by relation — finds the issue you half-remember.

## The full story is replayable

The ops-log holds every step: who changed what, when, and in what order. A post-mortem reads history instead of reconstructing it. An audit is a replay, not an interview. And when a projection is wrong or a new view is needed, it is rebuilt from the log rather than patched by hand.

## Humans follow without interrupting

Management sees what is being done, blocked, and researched on dashboards fed by the same API the agents use. Status meetings stop being the mechanism for finding out what is happening; the state of the work is simply visible.

## No ceremony

No board grooming, no workflow schemes, no ticket etiquette. The tracker adapts to how the work is actually done — by agents and people together — instead of the work bending to the tracker.

## And that is when it really begins

Because the headless core is the foundation. On top of it, teams build the views and automations only they know they need: a dashboard shaped to one team's flow, an agent that triages incoming reports, a digest that writes itself. They do not have to worry about the foundation — every action is recorded, every state is derivable, every capability is callable. What they build on it is their own craftsmanship.
